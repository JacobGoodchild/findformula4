## Deep dive 1 (17:37 UTC onward): Random target lemma (Aldous-Fill Cor 2.14; Kemeny's constant)
Theorem: sum_j pi_j E_i[T_j] is independent of start i. Chosen because it is central, taught, and a famous "surprising constant".
Generalisations tested (results/random_target/):
- **Second moment**: sum_j pi_j E_i[T_j^2] DOES depend on i (path P6: 216.5, 232.5, 248.5; lollipop, random graphs likewise). Derived why: with H2 the matrix of second moments, (I-P)H2 pi = (2K+1)*1 - (pi_j s_j)_j, where s_j = E_j[(return time)^2]; so it solves a Poisson equation with a non-constant right-hand side. Hence no start-independence. Variance version also depends on i.
- **Random target set** (pairs {j,k}, natural weights pi_j pi_k, pi_j+pi_k, uniform): all depend on start. Weights that do give start-independence exist (nullspace of dimension C(n,2)-(n-1)) but none is natural.
- Regression of pi'M2 pi on Kemeny-type spectral sums sum(1-lambda)^-p failed on non-reversible chains (needs return-time second moments), consistent with the derivation.
Literature (searches: random target lemma + higher moments; variance generalisation; Hunter variances paper; Levene-Loizou; Fitzsimmons proof): the first-moment lemma is classical; higher-moment formulas come from powers of the fundamental matrix Z (Hunter 2006 and Levene-Loizou), not from a start-independent identity. Active recent work: Kemeny constant for second-order walks (Fasino). Status: **dead / known territory**. Not logged as a result.

## Deep dive 2 (17:40 UTC): Cayley-Menger determinant of the effective-resistance matrix (classical Cayley-Menger + Kirchhoff matrix-tree)
Claim tested: for any connected graph on n vertices, the points with squared distances R_ij (effective resistances) form a simplex with CM-det(R) = (-1)^n 2^(n-1) / tau, i.e. volume = 1/((n-1)! sqrt(tau)). **Verified exactly (rational arithmetic) on all 994 connected graphs with 3..7 vertices**, 0 failures (results/resistance_simplex/cm.py). Proof is one line: the Gram matrix of the vectors x_i - x_0 is the inverse of the reduced Laplacian, whose determinant is 1/tau.
Also noted: the dictionary Laplacian entries <-> dihedral angles of the simplex (non-negative conductances iff non-obtuse simplex).
Literature: **KNOWN.** Devriendt & Van Mieghem, "The Simplex Geometry of Graphs" (arXiv:1807.06475, opened and read via pdftotext), Section 4.5 eq. (28) gives volume of the simplex in terms of the number of (weighted) spanning trees, "inspired by Fiedler's expression [17, Cor. 1.4.6]"; Fiedler's non-obtuse-simplex/M-matrix theory; Devriendt, "Effective resistance is more than distance: Laplacians, Simplices and the Schur complement" (arXiv:2010.04521, search hit only, not opened); Bapat's determinant of the resistance matrix (arXiv:2606.21902 hit only). Status: **known**, not recorded as a result.

## Deep dive 3 (17:42-): Resistance simplex circumsphere (classical Cayley-Menger/Fiedler geometry x Kirchhoff spanning trees)
Found by exploration: the points with squared distances = effective resistances lie on a sphere whose centre has barycentric coordinates lambda_i = 1 - (1/2) E[deg_T(i)] (T = random spanning tree), verified exactly (rational arithmetic) on all 995 connected graphs with 2..7 vertices and on ~100 random integer-weighted graphs (results/resistance_simplex/exact_circ.py).
Proved in 5 lines (results/circumsphere/statement_and_proof.md) from the identity L R = w 1^T - 2I.
Theorem B (formula): rho^2 = kf/n^2 + (1/(4n^2)) sum_edges c_ij (F_i - F_j)^2, F_i = sum_k R_ik; |circumcentre - centroid|^2 = the second term. Verified exactly on the same set.
Numerics (353 graphs up to n=40): for unit weights (n-1)/n^2 <= rho^2 <= (n-1)/4, equality at K_n (lower) and at every tree (upper); NOT yet proved for the upper bound.
Literature check: **Theorem A is KNOWN in substance.** lambda_i = 1 - (1/2) sum_j c_ij R_ij is exactly the "node resistance curvature" of Devriendt & Lambiotte (arXiv:2201.06385; eq (8): p_i = E[p_i(T)] over a random spanning tree, opened), "Graphs with nonnegative resistance curvature" (arXiv:2410.07756, opened: "expected degree at most two in a random spanning tree"), and circumcentre/circumradius via Fiedler's identity are in Devriendt arXiv:2010.04521 Thm 2 (opened) with Fiedler Cor 1.4.13; Devriendt arXiv:2405.20300 (opened) uses circumcentre and Lemoine point to give Kemeny's constant K = R^2 - |gamma - l|^2 and asks what gamma_hat >= 0 means for the chain. So A and consequence (ii) are known. Theorem B's closed formula for rho^2 / centroid distance via farness differences was not found in what I opened (unverified: not claimed new). Status: **known (A), minor/possibly new (B), not a headline result.**
Update 17:50: referee (fresh agent, saw only statement+proof) found A, B, (i), (ii) correct; two small fixes applied (centroid normalisation in step 6, proof of (iii) supplied by referee). New: Theorem C (4 rho^2 = Dirichlet energy of R(i,.) for every i, from L R = w 1^T - 2I) and bound rho^2 <= (1/4) sum_E c R^2 <= (1/4) sum_E R_e, equality iff tree; unit weights (n-1)/n^2 <= rho^2 <= (n-1)/4.
