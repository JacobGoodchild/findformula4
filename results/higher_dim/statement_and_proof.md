# Face-degree law for Kalai's random d-tree of the simplex (statement + proof)

Setting. n >= d+2. X = the (n-1)-simplex on vertices [n]; its d-faces are the (d+1)-subsets of [n], its (d-1)-skeleton is complete.
A d-tree (d-dimensional spanning tree) T is a set of d-faces with H_d(T)=0 and H_{d-1}(T;Z) finite (Kalai; Duval-Klivans-Martin; Lyons "k-bases").
Kalai: sum_T |H_{d-1}(T)|^2 = n^{C(n-2,d)}. Random tree: P(T) = |H_{d-1}(T)|^2 / n^{C(n-2,d)}  (Lyons's "d-th lower matroidal measure": P(T)=det(del_{S,T}^* del_{S,T})/det(del_{S,all}^* del_{S,all}); it is the determinantal probability measure on d-faces with kernel Pi = orthogonal projection of C_d onto the coboundary space im(del_d^*), where del_d: C_d -> C_{d-1} is the boundary. d=1: uniform spanning tree of K_n.)

THEOREM. Fix 0 <= j <= d-1 and a j-face tau (a (j+1)-subset of [n]). Let N be the number of d-faces of the random tree T that contain tau. Then
    N  =  C(n-j-2, d-j-1)  +  Bin( C(n-j-2, d-j), (j+1)/n )      (in distribution; the binomial is independent of nothing else - i.e. N - C(n-j-2,d-j-1) is binomial).
Special cases: d=1,j=0: degree of a vertex in a uniform labelled tree is 1 + Bin(n-2, 1/n) (Cayley/Prufer, classical). j=0, general d: C(n-2,d-1) + Bin(C(n-2,d), 1/n), which is the vertex-degree law contained in Kalai's weighted formula tau_d(Delta_{n,d}; v) = (v_1...v_n)^{C(n-2,d-1)} (v_1+...+v_n)^{C(n-2,d)} [Duval-Klivans-Martin, arXiv:1506.06819, eq (20), quoted from Kalai Thm 3']. j = d-1 (ridges): N = 1 + Bin(n-d-1, d/n).  For 1 <= j <= d-1 the law is, as far as I could find, not stated in the literature (see NOVELTY.md).

PROOF.
1. Determinantal facts (Lyons 2003; see arXiv 0811.2933 sec 2): for the determinantal measure with projection kernel Pi, and any finite set S of d-faces, the number N_S = |T cap S| has probability generating function E z^{N_S} = det(I - (1-z) Pi_S), where Pi_S is the principal submatrix. Hence if Pi_S has eigenvalues mu_1..mu_s then N_S is a sum of independent Bernoulli(mu_i) (Bernoulli(1) = constant 1).
2. Pi for the complete skeleton. Let B = del_d (rows: (d-1)-faces, columns: d-faces, entries +-1). For the complete complex on n vertices, the up- and down-Laplacians satisfy del_d del_d^* + del_{d-1}^* del_{d-1} = n I on C_{d-1}  (Kalai; DKM arXiv:1506.06819 section 4: all non-trivial Laplacian eigenvalues of the simplex equal n). On im(del_d) = ker(del_{d-1}) (complete skeleton is acyclic) this gives del_d del_d^* = n I, so Pi = B^*(BB^*)^+ B = (1/n) B^* B.  I.e. Pi_{sigma,sigma'} = (1/n) <del sigma, del sigma'>.
3. Star of tau. S = {sigma = tau u rho : rho a (d-j)-subset of [n]\tau}. Orient sigma as [tau, rho]; changing orientations conjugates Pi_S by a diagonal +-1 matrix, which does not change spectrum or determinants of principal minors. Faces of del sigma: (a) those omitting a vertex x of tau: tau\x u rho; two different sigma, sigma' in S never share such a face (it determines rho), so (a) contributes only to the diagonal, (j+1) in total; (b) those omitting a vertex y of rho: tau u (rho\y), with coefficient (-1)^{j+1} * (coefficient of rho\y in del_link rho). Hence for sigma != sigma' in S: <del sigma, del sigma'> = <del_L rho, del_L rho'>, where del_L is the boundary map of the COMPLETE complex L on the m = n-j-1 vertices [n]\tau, in dimension p = d-j-1 (rho has p+1 vertices). Therefore
      n * Pi_S  =  (j+1) I  +  del_L^* del_L   (on C_p(L), dimension C(m,p+1) = C(n-j-1,d-j) = |S|).
4. Spectrum of del_L^* del_L on the complete complex L on m vertices: nonzero eigenvalues equal those of del_L del_L^* on im del_L, which is m (by step 2 applied to L), with multiplicity rank(del_L) = C(m-1,p) = C(n-j-2,d-j-1); the rest is the kernel, of dimension C(m,p+1) - C(m-1,p) = C(m-1,p+1) = C(n-j-2,d-j).
5. So Pi_S has eigenvalue ((j+1)+m)/n = 1 with multiplicity C(n-j-2,d-j-1) and eigenvalue (j+1)/n with multiplicity C(n-j-2,d-j). By step 1, N = C(n-j-2,d-j-1) + Bin(C(n-j-2,d-j),(j+1)/n). QED.

Checks. Numerical (floating point) verification of the predicted spectrum of Pi_S for (d,j,n) in {(2,0,5..7),(2,1,5..7),(3,0,6..7),(3,1,6..8),(3,2,6..8),(4,2,7..8)}: max error ~1e-15 (results/higher_dim/kalai_j.py), and of the full degree distribution for d=1..3 (kalai_d.py).
Consequences: E N = C(n-j-2,d-j-1) + (j+1)C(n-j-2,d-j)/n; Var N = C(n-j-2,d-j) (j+1)(n-j-1)/n^2. For d=2, j=1 (edges in a random 2-tree): the expected fraction of edges lying in exactly one triangle -> e^{-2} as n -> infinity, vs 1/e for vertices in trees.
